17 Kitchens, One Overnight: What That Actually Looks Like

On the overnight of August 31, Jorge Garcia ran 17 kitchens in a single shift at a major venue. In at 11:46 PM. Out at 8:00 AM. Two cleaning groups running simultaneously, one person dedicated entirely to oil collection supporting both.

What most people don't see in a number like "17 kitchens, one overnight" is the logistics. You're not running 17 kitchens sequentially. You're running them in parallel, managing crew movement across floors, tracking oil output per location, ensuring each location's documentation is complete before moving to the next, and maintaining the same standard at kitchen 17 that you held at kitchen 1.

That requirement, report filed before the crew leaves, is what forces the standard. You cannot close out a location without the documentation. The system won't submit incomplete records. Every location gets completed to standard or it doesn't get checked off.

Carmarion Malone completed three inspections out of three this week, the second consecutive week at that completion rate.

The Documentation System Behind Consistent Results

The field inspection system that makes this possible has three components that work together:

1. The Tablet in Every Van

Every TruShine van runs with a field inspection tablet. The rule is simple: no tablet, no job start. The documentation system is not optional equipment, it's the job. A crew arriving at a location without the tablet is a crew that cannot complete the job to standard.

2. Per-Location, Per-Fryer Documentation

The system requires per-fryer documentation, not per-location documentation. Each fryer is individually logged with filtered/recycled status, pound weight where applicable, and flagged for low oil or replenishment needs. Before/after photos are required at each location before the location can be marked complete.

3. Automatic Report Generation and Delivery

When a location is marked complete, the report generates automatically, timestamped, photo-documented, with fryer counts, oil out data, supervisor name, and time in/time out. It delivers to the client's designated contacts, chef, AP coordinator, facilities manager, sustainability team, before the crew leaves the building.

This is the product. Not the cleaning. The certainty that the cleaning happened, documented to the standard required for any inspector, available immediately on request.

NFPA 96 fire suppression certifications were completed and filed for two accounts this week. Both kitchens are now fully documented with current fire suppression certifications. Both clients received their certification records before our crew left the building.

📋 NFPA 96 Fire Certification: What's Required

Under NFPA 96, commercial kitchen fire suppression systems require semi-annual inspection and certification. The certification must document the inspection date, the certifying company, the technician credentials, and the specific components inspected. TruShine generates digital certification records automatically at the completion of every fire suppression inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does TruShine maintain the same standard across multiple states?
Three systems work together: a standardized field inspection tablet in every van that cannot be bypassed, per-location per-fryer documentation requirements that must be completed before a job closes, and automatic report generation that delivers to the client before the crew leaves. The documentation requirement enforces the standard, you cannot close a location without completing it to spec.
What is the NFPA 96 fire certification process?
Under NFPA 96, commercial kitchen fire suppression systems require semi-annual inspection by a certified technician. The inspection covers nozzle condition, agent levels, fusible links, and system integrity. TruShine generates a digital certification record automatically at completion, documenting the inspection date, technician credentials, components inspected, and compliance status, delivered to the client before the crew leaves the building.
Can TruShine service multi-location operators across multiple states?
Yes. TruShine operates across Georgia, Florida, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Alabama, and Virginia. Multi-location operators receive one compliance calendar covering all locations across all states, one documentation system generating consistent records, one account manager, and one invoice. The standard is the same regardless of which state or how many locations.
What documentation does a restaurant receive after each service?
Every service generates a CASR (Cleaning and Service Record) report with per-fryer documentation, before/after photos with timestamps, supervisor name and certification, service scope, oil out data where applicable, and compliance notes. The report delivers automatically to designated contacts, chef, AP coordinator, sustainability team, before the crew leaves. Records are retained for 3 years.
What services are covered under a TruShine compliance program?
TruShine covers all eight commercial kitchen compliance categories: Exhaust Hood System (NFPA 96), Fire Suppression (NFPA 17A), Grease Trap Service (FOG compliance), UCO Collection and Recycling (EPA licensed), Oil Filtration via Fry360°, Commercial Deep Cleaning, Janitorial Program, and Drain Jetting. All eight services are available under one contract, one compliance calendar, and one invoice.
