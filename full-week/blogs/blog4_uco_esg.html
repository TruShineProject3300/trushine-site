In 2025, the largest US commercial UCO collector — serving over 45,000 foodservice locations including major national restaurant brands — recycled nearly 393 million pounds of used cooking oil, converting it to renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel through partnerships with major petroleum refiners.

UCO commands a market premium because it qualifies as a waste feedstock under US and EU regulations, making UCO-based biofuels eligible for premium tax credits — including the 45Z credit of $1.75 per gallon for sustainable aviation fuel — and double-counting under EU renewable energy directives. The carbon intensity of UCO-based fuel is 70–90% lower than fossil jet fuel, making it the feedstock of choice for airlines meeting sustainability compliance obligations.

Your kitchen is already part of this supply chain. The question is whether you're in it with the right documentation.

What Happens to Your Oil After Collection

♻ The UCO Supply Chain

Step 1: Licensed collector removes UCO from your kitchen and issues a chain-of-custody manifest.
Step 2: Oil is transported to a licensed processing facility — biodiesel refinery or renewable fuel producer.
Step 3: UCO is converted to biodiesel, renewable diesel (R99), or sustainable aviation fuel (SAF).
Step 4: Fuel enters the transportation supply chain, reducing carbon intensity by up to 75% vs fossil fuel.
Step 5: Collection data is available for your ESG, sustainability, or CSR reporting — volume, carbon reduction, and disposal chain.

This is a genuine circular economy. The oil that cooked food in your kitchen this week is in a fuel tank somewhere in the country next month. The only thing that determines whether your kitchen participates cleanly — or creates liability — is whether the collector is licensed and the chain of custody is documented.

Why Free Pickup Is a Legal Trap

This is the mistake that costs restaurant operators the most: accepting UCO pickup from an unlicensed collector because it's free or generates a small revenue share, without verifying the collector's license, EPA compliance, and manifest documentation.

"Unlicensed UCO collection — even free pickup — is an environmental violation that attaches to your kitchen as the waste generator. The violation is yours, not the hauler's."

Under federal and state environmental law, you are the waste generator. If someone collects your used cooking oil without a license, without chain-of-custody documentation, and disposes of it improperly — you bear the environmental liability. Your restaurant. Your permit. Your fine. The unlicensed hauler may be long gone.

Additionally, UCO disposal documentation is part of the FOG compliance record your wastewater utility inspector checks. A missing or incomplete chain-of-custody record is a citable FOG violation — separate from and in addition to any grease trap violations.

⚠ What to Verify Before Your Next UCO Pickup

✓ Is the collector licensed and permitted — verifiable with your state environmental authority?
✓ Do they issue a chain-of-custody manifest at every collection?
✓ Is volume documented in gallons or pounds per visit?
✓ Do they provide disposal facility documentation confirming licensed processing?
✓ Are records retained and available for FOG inspection or ESG reporting?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the market value of used cooking oil in 2026?
UCO traded at $1,000–1,400 per tonne CIF ARA in Q1 2026, driven by biodiesel blending mandates, sustainable aviation fuel demand, and the 45Z tax credit. The global formal UCO market reached an estimated 4.5–5 million tonnes in 2026.
Is unlicensed free UCO pickup legal?
No. Unlicensed used cooking oil collection — even free pickup — is an environmental violation that attaches to your kitchen as the waste generator. Under federal and state environmental law, you are responsible for the proper disposal of waste your kitchen generates. If an unlicensed hauler disposes improperly, the violation and fines attach to your restaurant.
What documentation does licensed UCO collection require?
A permitted collector verifiable with your state environmental authority, a chain-of-custody manifest at each collection, volume documentation in gallons or pounds, and disposal facility documentation confirming licensed processing. How does UCO collection relate to FOG compliance?

Unlicensed pickup leaves a gap that a FOG inspector can cite as a violation — separate from any grease trap violations.
