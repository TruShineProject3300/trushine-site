The Agency You Don't Know About

FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. Your municipal wastewater utility enforces FOG compliance, not your health department. They are two different government agencies that do not coordinate with each other. Their inspectors do not share findings. Their inspection schedules do not align. And their violation records are maintained in completely separate systems.

⚠ The Gap That Catches Operators Every Year

You can have a perfect health inspection: 100 out of 100, zero violations, and receive a FOG citation the same week from a completely different inspector looking at completely different records. The health inspector checks kitchen cleanliness. The FOG inspector checks your grease trap pump-out manifests, your 3-year documentation history, your UCO disposal chain, and whether a licensed contractor signed every record.

In 2026, Miami-Dade's automated FOG portal cross-checks pump-out manifests nightly. A log exceeding 90 days without service triggers a $250 administrative fee before any inspector ever visits, and then the inspector visits. A single Florida restaurant received 70 violations in April 2026, the most of any restaurant in the state that year, with grease trap documentation as a primary contributor.

One FOG citation can put both compliance tracks at risk simultaneously.

What the FOG Inspector Actually Checks

When a FOG inspector arrives at your kitchen, they are not checking how clean it looks. They are checking whether you can produce specific documents immediately. Here is exactly what they ask for:

  • Grease trap pump-out manifests - covering the last 3 years, retained on premises at the kitchen, not at the vendor's office. If the documents are with your vendor, you don't have them.
  • Pumping frequency records - documenting compliance with the 90-day maximum service cycle. If your last pump-out was 91 days ago, that's a violation regardless of trap fill level.
  • The 25% rule - if combined grease and solids reach 25% of the trap's liquid capacity, the trap must be serviced immediately. Inspectors carry sampling tools and measure in real time.
  • Licensed UCO disposal documentation - proof that used cooking oil was collected by a licensed contractor with chain-of-custody documentation. Free unlicensed pickup creates liability that attaches to your kitchen.
  • Grease discharge permit - annual permit from your local water authority confirming your interceptor was reviewed and approved for your operation's flow rate.

Missing any of these is an automatic violation, regardless of how clean the kitchen looks or whether the service was actually performed.

The Fire Marshal: A Third Inspector

Your fire marshal is the third inspector, and the 2025 NFPA 96 update changed the compliance threshold in ways most operators haven't heard about yet.

⚠ 2025 NFPA 96: What Changed

New monthly requirement: Any kitchen running 16+ hours per day now requires monthly hood cleaning, regardless of fuel type. Previously these operations qualified for quarterly cleaning. Hotels, late-night restaurants, 24-hour diners, and multi-event venue kitchens are all affected.

Documentation standard elevated: Digital before/after photos with timestamps are now required after every cleaning following NFPA 96. A sticker on the hood is no longer sufficient documentation in the event of an inspection or insurance claim.

Certificate content expanded: Must include technician certification details, specific areas serviced, and date, not just company name and date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who enforces FOG compliance in restaurants?
Your municipal wastewater utility, not your health department. These are two completely separate government agencies that do not coordinate inspections. You can pass your health inspection and fail your FOG inspection on the same day.
What does the FOG inspector check?
Grease trap pump-out manifests covering the last 3 years (must be retained on premises), evidence the trap was serviced within the 90-day maximum cycle, licensed UCO disposal documentation with chain-of-custody, and whether the trap's grease and solid level exceeds 25% of capacity.
What are the fines for FOG violations in 2026?
Miami-Dade triggers a $250 automatic fee for a grease trap exceeding 90 days without service before any inspector visits. First-offense unlawful grease disposal in Florida starts at $2,500. Operating without a required grease trap results in $500–$25,000 fines plus possible closure. Federal Clean Water Act violations can reach $25,000 per day per violation.
Can a FOG violation trigger a health department reinspection?
Yes. FOG violations are sometimes referred to the local health department, which can trigger a separate inspection of your food service license. One FOG citation can put both compliance tracks at risk simultaneously.
What changed in the 2025 NFPA 96 update?
Any operation running more than 16 hours per day now requires monthly hood cleaning regardless of fuel type. Digital before/after photos with timestamps are now required documentation, a sticker alone no longer meets the standard. Both changes are actively enforced in 2026.
