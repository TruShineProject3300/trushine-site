Two offices

Marietta hood files have to name the right fire authority

Restaurant groups treat "Marietta" as one market. Fire inspections do not. A storefront on the Square is a city inspection. A kitchen on a road that still uses a Marietta ZIP, east of the city boundary, is often a Cobb County inspection. Showing up with paperwork addressed to the wrong office wastes the visit even when the metal is clean.

The buildings differ too. Around the Square, crews deal with low roofs, awkward ladder landings, and fans that discharge near windows. Farther out, the hoods get longer, the roofs get flatter, and the weekend volume at family dining rooms loads filters faster than a weekday cafe. Scheduling both on a six-month habit is how the busy one fails first.

Georgia code

The state table, applied in Cobb

Georgia's Safety Fire Commissioner rules, Chapter 120-3-3, are how the state adopts its fire codes, including NFPA 96's subject matter. The state fire prevention code's commercial cooking section (Section 606 in the edition based on the 2024 International Fire Code) sets an inspection rhythm by cooking type, then requires cleaning when grease has accumulated. Atlanta's page walks the four rows in full. The short version for a Marietta operator:

  • Solid fuel: look every month.
  • Charbroiling, wok, or 24-hour cooking: look every 3 months.
  • A typical full-service or fast-casual line: look every 6 months.
  • A church hall, a seasonal kitchen, or a senior-center cafeteria: look every 12 months.

That is the published state table, not a Cobb invention, and your inspector can ask for more. Read the longer note on the Atlanta NFPA 96 page and then confirm the edition with Marietta Fire or the Cobb County fire marshal, whichever one actually inspects you.

Questions

Marietta hood cleaning questions

Which fire marshal covers a Marietta kitchen?

If the building is inside the City of Marietta, start with the Marietta Fire Department. If the address is unincorporated Cobb County, including much of what people call East Cobb, the Cobb County fire marshal is the usual authority. When a mailing address is ambiguous, the county parcel or the fire station that inspects you is the tie-breaker.

Does Cobb use a different hood rule than Atlanta?

Both sit under Georgia's state fire code. The inspection table is the same shape: monthly for solid fuel, every 3 months for high-volume, charbroiler, and wok cooking, every 6 months for other restaurants, every 12 months for low-volume uses. Cleaning follows when grease is found. Local inspectors can tighten that.

Are Marietta Square hoods harder to clean?

Often, yes. The Square and the streets just off it have older roofs, short duct runs, and fans close to neighboring buildings. Access is the constraint, not the chemistry. We write down any section we cannot reach.

What about East Cobb and the Kennesaw side?

East Cobb hoods are frequently larger and busier on weekends. Kennesaw, Smyrna, Acworth, and Powder Springs are nearby and can share a route. They are not all inside the City of Marietta, so do not assume one sticker format satisfies every Cobb city.

Do you replace the suppression inspection?

No. Hood cleaning and the wet-chemical system are different visits. A Marietta kitchen can be current on one and late on the other.
