The inspector

Raleigh Fire looks at hoods. Wake County may be someone else.

The useful local fact is narrow and official: Raleigh's fire department inspects restaurant hoods as part of fire protection for buildings in the city. Operators sometimes hear "NFPA 96" from that visit and go looking for a vendor who will only wash filters. The inspector is looking at the exhaust path, which includes the duct and the fan, and at whether you can show a date.

The metro makes the authority messy. Glenwood South is the city. A kitchen in Cary with a Raleigh-area manager is not. Garner, Morrisville, Wake Forest, and Knightdale each deserve a one-line check on who walks in. We would rather ask than print the wrong department on a report.

Health inspections are a second calendar. They care about food, temperatures, and cleanliness around the line. They are not a substitute for the hood record, and the hood record is not a substitute for them.

State code

Same North Carolina rule Charlotte uses, different streets

Raleigh's ordinances require commercial buildings to follow the North Carolina Fire Prevention Code. That code is how NFPA 96 shows up in a Raleigh kitchen. The hood inspection schedule is about checking grease levels on the hood and duct parts. Suppression maintenance is a different matter. If Raleigh has moved to a newer code edition, the section number may have changed and the distinction is the part to keep.

Read the Charlotte write-up if you want that distinction in more detail: hood cleaning in Charlotte. Then ask the Raleigh fire marshal, or Wake County if you are outside the city, which edition they are carrying. We will clean to the interval your menu and that office require. We will not invent a Raleigh amendment we have not read.

Questions

Raleigh hood cleaning questions

Does Raleigh inspect restaurant hoods?

Yes. The city's fire department describes restaurant hoods as part of its inspection work, along with alarms, sprinklers, and other fire systems. Inside the city, that is the office to plan for. The Raleigh code of ordinances also points commercial buildings at the North Carolina Fire Prevention Code.

What about Cary, Apex, and the rest of Wake County?

Cary and Apex run their own fire departments. Unincorporated Wake County is generally the Wake County Fire Marshal. A kitchen that staff call Raleigh because of the metro can still be someone else's inspection. Check the district before you file a sticker under the wrong name.

How often is cleaning due?

Follow the North Carolina adoption of NFPA 96 practice, and confirm the edition. The familiar rows are monthly for solid fuel, quarterly for high-volume char or wok and 24-hour cooking, semiannual for moderate restaurants, and annual for low-volume halls. The hood inspection schedule is about checking grease levels to see if cleaning is needed. It is not the suppression-service clock. See the Charlotte page for that distinction, then verify the table number locally.

How is Glenwood South different from a North Hills kitchen?

Glenwood South and the Warehouse District run late. North Hills, Brier Creek, and Crabtree-area kitchens often combine shopping-center volume with more ordinary roofs. Downtown weekday kitchens track the state-office lunch rush. The cleaning steps match. The interval and the roof plan should not.

Do you handle the health inspection too?

No. Wake County environmental health inspects food handling on its own schedule. We clean and document the exhaust system the fire marshal is asking about. We do not prepare a health-department score.
