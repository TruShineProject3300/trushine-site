Drive from Downtown to West Midtown and the hoods change with the buildings. Downtown and Midtown stacks rise through several floors before they reach a fan. NFPA 96

Inspection frequency, what gets checked, and the hood label

NFPA 96 is the standard for ventilation control and fire protection of commercial cooking operations. It tells a kitchen how often to inspect the exhaust system for grease buildup, and it says to clean the parts that are contaminated. Inside the city of Atlanta, Atlanta Fire Rescue is the fire authority. Decatur, Sandy Springs, Marietta, Alpharetta, and Norcross each sit with their own authority. The standard does not change at the county line. The person who asks to see the label does.

The inspection clock follows the cooking, not the ZIP code:

CookingNFPA 96 inspection
Solid fuelEvery month
High volume, including 24-hour cooking, charbroiling, and wok cookingEvery 3 months
Moderate volumeEvery 6 months
Low volume, such as churches, day camps, seasonal businesses, and senior centersEvery 12 months

If that inspection finds grease deposits, the contaminated portions of the hood, the filters, the duct, and the fan are cleaned. An inspector can require a shorter interval for a particular kitchen. Two Atlanta stores with the same logo do not automatically share a calendar. A charbroiler and a kitchen that only reheats are not the same volume.

What an inspector checks

When the visit is judged against NFPA 96, the inspector is looking at grease, access, and paperwork:

  • Grease on the hood, the filters or other grease-removal devices, the plenum, the duct, and the fan.
  • Whether access panels open, and whether a section that could not be reached is disclosed.
  • Whether the fan hinge works and the wheel was actually cleaned when the roof was safe to work.
  • The hood label and the written report on the premises.
  • The suppression tag, which is a separate system and does not stand in for the hood record.

Service report and hood label

The visit

What an Atlanta hood cleaning includes

Canopy and filters

Baffle filters come out and get washed. The canopy and plenum are degreased. Mesh filters, where a hood still has them, get called out because they are a poor grease stop.

Accessible duct

The duct is cleaned through the panels that exist. Older in-town buildings sometimes have a riser with no safe opening. That limit goes in the report.

Rooftop fan

When the roof is safe to work, the fan is hinged, the wheel and the housing are cleaned, and the hinge is left working. A shiny canopy with a loaded fan is not a finished hood.

Protection of the line

Nearby surfaces are covered before degreaser is used. Appliances have to be shut down. Night and weekend starts are available when the line has to cook the next morning.

Hood label and report

You get a hood label and a written report with TruShine Services, the person who did the work, and the date. Sections that were not cleaned are named.

A straight scope

A filter wash is not described as a full system cleaning. If the fan was locked out or the roof was unsafe, the report says the fan was not cleaned.

Suppression nozzles, links, and cylinders are not part of this hood cleaning. Those belong to a suppression inspection. See fire suppression if that tag is the item that is about to lapse.

Atlanta hood cleaning questions

How often does NFPA 96 say to inspect an Atlanta hood?

NFPA 96 sets the inspection clock by cooking volume, not by the city name. Solid-fuel cooking is inspected every month. High-volume cooking, including 24-hour cooking, charbroiling, and wok cooking, is inspected every 3 months. Moderate-volume cooking is inspected every 6 months. Low-volume cooking, such as churches, day camps, seasonal businesses, and senior centers, is inspected every 12 months. If the inspection finds grease deposits, the contaminated portions are cleaned. The fire inspector can require a shorter interval for a particular kitchen.

What does an inspector check on a hood cleaning?

On an NFPA 96 hood visit, the inspector is looking for grease on the hood, the filters or other grease-removal devices, the plenum, the duct, and the fan. They look at whether access panels open, whether the fan hinge works, and whether any section that could not be reached is written down. They also look for the hood label and the written service report on the premises. The suppression tag is a separate document.
