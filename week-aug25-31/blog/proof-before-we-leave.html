Why "We Did The Job" Is Not Enough

The Inspector Problem

Commercial kitchen compliance is enforced by multiple agencies with different documentation requirements. Your health department needs evidence of cleaning frequency and sanitation standards. Your fire marshal needs hood service records following NFPA 96 for your hood system. Your wastewater authority needs pump-out manifests for your grease trap, retained for a minimum of three years in most Southeast jurisdictions.

When an inspector arrives unannounced, and in active enforcement markets like Atlanta, Nashville, and Charlotte, they do arrive unannounced, you need to produce documentation on the spot. From every vendor. For every service. Immediately.

If your grease trap was pumped but the manifest is at your vendor's office, you're in violation. If your hood was cleaned but the certification is on a sticker that got wiped off during a deep clean, you're in violation. If your UCO was collected by an unlicensed hauler because they offered free pickup, you're in violation, regardless of how clean your fryers look.

The Five-Vendor Problem

Most restaurant operators manage 4-6 separate compliance vendors. Each one maintains their own documentation in their own format on their own timeline. When an inspector asks for records across all categories simultaneously, you're scrambling through five separate systems under pressure.

One consolidated vendor with one documentation system means one source. All current. All accessible. All formatted for inspection.

The Compliance Risk Most Operators Don't Know About

Unlicensed UCO pickup, even free pickup, is an environmental violation that creates liability for your operation. The violation attaches to the generator of the waste (your kitchen), not the hauler. If someone is picking up your used cooking oil without a license and disposal manifests, you're exposed.

Every TruShine Environmental collection includes licensed disposal, a manifest documenting the chain of custody from your fryer to the processing facility, and integration with your CASR report so the documentation is complete and inspection-ready.

What Happens When the Inspector Walks In

Active enforcement markets in the Southeast run scheduled compliance audits, not just complaint-driven inspections. Atlanta, Nashville, and Charlotte are all active enforcement markets for FOG (fats, oils, and grease) compliance in 2026.

When a wastewater inspector walks into your kitchen unannounced, they will ask for:

  1. Grease trap pump-out manifests - covering the last 3 years
  2. Pumping frequency records - documenting compliance with the 90-day maximum in most Southeast jurisdictions
  3. Licensed collector verification - proof that your UCO and grease disposal was handled by a licensed contractor

A TruShine client produces all three immediately, because every service generates the documentation automatically and it's maintained in one accessible system.

📋 Active Enforcement Markets in 2026

Atlanta, Nashville, and Charlotte conduct scheduled FOG compliance audits. Fines in Florida reach $10,000 per day for water quality violations. Missing pump-out records is an automatic violation, regardless of whether the trap was actually serviced.

Your Kitchen Documentation Checklist

Use this checklist to assess whether your current vendor setup is producing the documentation you need for any inspection:

  • ☐ Do you receive a service report after every hood cleaning, with date, technician name, and service following NFPA 96?
  • ☐ Do you receive a grease trap pump-out manifest after every service, with licensed disposal documentation?
  • ☐ Are your manifests retained for 3 years minimum?
  • ☐ Do you have a record of UCO collection by a licensed contractor, not just free pickup?
  • ☐ Do you receive photo documentation of each service location?
  • ☐ Is your fire suppression inspection current on the semi-annual cycle described in NFPA 17A?
  • ☐ Is all documentation accessible immediately, not stored at a vendor's office?

If you checked fewer than 5 of these, you have compliance documentation gaps that an inspector would find.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a CASR report and when do I receive it?
A CASR (Cleaning and Service Record) is TruShine's digital documentation of every service performed. It includes the service date and time, supervisor name, fryer count, pounds recycled, before/after photos, and compliance notes. It is delivered automatically to your designated recipients, chef, AP coordinator, sustainability team, before our crew leaves your building. Not the next day. Not next week. The same shift.
How long are service records retained?
TruShine retains all CASR reports and disposal manifests for a minimum of 3 years, meeting the documentation retention requirement for grease trap pump-outs in all Southeast jurisdictions. Records are accessible immediately upon request.
What is the environmental impact of UCO recycling?
Used cooking oil collected by TruShine Environmental is processed into biodiesel, animal feed, and industrial base products. This week alone, our collection of 10,370 pounds resulted in 9 metric tons of CO₂ avoided, equivalent to 414 trees, or 2,228 gallons of gasoline diverted from the waste stream. Every collection includes documented environmental impact metrics.
Is free UCO pickup compliant?
No. Unlicensed UCO collection, even free collection, is an environmental violation that attaches to the generator of the waste, which is your kitchen. The violation is yours, not the hauler's. Every TruShine Environmental collection is performed by licensed collectors with documented chain-of-custody manifests.
