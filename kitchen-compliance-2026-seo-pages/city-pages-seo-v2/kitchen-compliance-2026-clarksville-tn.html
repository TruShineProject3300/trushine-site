NFPA 96 Requirements for Clarksville Kitchens

NFPA 96 is the national fire safety standard for commercial kitchens. It governs hood cleaning frequency, documentation requirements, fire suppression inspection schedules, and exhaust system maintenance. Every commercial kitchen in Clarksville is subject to NFPA 96, regardless of size or cooking volume.

What Changed in the 2025 NFPA 96 Update

The 2025 update introduced two significant changes affecting Clarksville kitchen operators:

  • Expanded monthly cleaning requirements - any operation running more than 16 hours per day now requires monthly hood cleaning, regardless of fuel type. This is a new requirement that catches high-volume operators who previously qualified for quarterly cleaning.
  • Digital documentation now required - timestamped before/after photos are now the standard after every certified cleaning. A service sticker on the hood is no longer sufficient documentation in the event of an inspection or dispute.

Hood Cleaning Frequency by Operation Type in Clarksville

  • Solid fuel operations (wood, charcoal): Monthly, no exceptions
  • High-volume commercial operations: Quarterly minimum
  • Operations running 16+ hours per day: Monthly (new 2025 requirement)
  • Low-volume operations: Semi-annually, with certified inspection determining eligibility

Clarksville FOG Compliance: The Second Inspector

This is the compliance gap that catches more Clarksville restaurant operators off guard than any other: FOG (fats, oils, and grease) compliance is enforced by Clarksville Gas and Water Department - a completely separate agency from your health department.

These are two different inspectors, from two different government agencies, who do not coordinate with each other. You can pass your Clarksville health inspection and fail your FOG inspection on the same day.

⚠ What the FOG Inspector Checks in Clarksville

When a Clarksville Gas and Water Department inspector arrives at your Clarksville kitchen, they will ask for:

• Grease trap pump-out manifests, covering the last 3 years, retained on premises
• Pumping frequency records, documenting compliance with the 90-day maximum cycle
• Licensed UCO disposal documentation, proof your used cooking oil was collected by a licensed contractor

Missing any of these is an automatic violation, regardless of how clean your kitchen looks or whether the work was actually done.

The UCO Licensing Requirement

Unlicensed used cooking oil collection, even free pickup, is an environmental violation that attaches to your kitchen as the waste generator. If someone collects your UCO without a license and chain-of-custody documentation, the violation is yours. TruShine Environmental handles licensed UCO collection in Clarksville with full documentation delivered before our truck leaves your dock.

Top Violations Resulting in Fines in Clarksville in 2026

  1. Missing hood cleaning certificate - cleaning was done but no NFPA 96 certificate with date, company, and areas serviced is on premises
  2. Overdue grease trap service - exceeded the 90-day maximum pumping cycle required by Clarksville Gas and Water Department
  3. Missing pump-out manifests - grease trap was serviced but records are at the vendor's office, not on your premises as required
  4. Unlicensed UCO disposal - used cooking oil collected by a non-licensed hauler without chain-of-custody documentation
  5. Expired fire suppression certification - semi-annual NFPA 17A inspection is overdue
  6. Insufficient hood cleaning documentation - missing before/after photos with timestamps per 2025 NFPA 96 update
  7. Repeat violations - prior citations not corrected, triggering escalated enforcement and higher fines

Frequently Asked Questions: Clarksville Kitchen Compliance

How often does a commercial kitchen hood need to be cleaned in Clarksville?
Under NFPA 96, hood cleaning frequency in Clarksville depends on your cooking volume and fuel type. Solid fuel operations require monthly cleaning. High-volume operations and any kitchen running more than 16 hours per day (new 2025 requirement) require quarterly cleaning. Low-volume operations may qualify for semi-annual cleaning. A certified inspection determines the correct frequency for your Clarksville kitchen. TruShine provides this assessment free of charge.
How often does a grease trap need to be pumped in Tennessee?
Most Tennessee jurisdictions require grease trap pumping every 90 days maximum, regardless of the trap's apparent fill level. The Clarksville Gas and Water Department enforces this requirement in Clarksville independently from the health department. All pump-out manifests must be retained on premises for a minimum of 3 years and must be produced immediately upon inspection request.
What documentation do I need after a hood cleaning in Clarksville?
Under the 2025 NFPA 96 update, every certified hood cleaning in Clarksville must produce a certificate showing the date of service, the cleaning company name, technician certification details, and the specific areas serviced. Digital documentation with before/after timestamps is now the standard. Every TruShine service generates a digital CASR report delivered to your team before our crew leaves your building.
Is free UCO pickup compliant in Clarksville?
No. Unlicensed used cooking oil collection, even free pickup, is an environmental violation that attaches to your kitchen as the waste generator. The violation is yours, not the hauler's.
