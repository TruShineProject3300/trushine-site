The fryer

Atlanta fryer oil needs its own container and its own pickup

The restaurants that search this term are usually staring at a full caddy, not at a fire sticker. West Midtown, Buckhead, and the corridors that fry all afternoon produce a steady stream of spent oil. Downtown alleys are a worse place to store it: shared space, a storm drain nearby, and a landlord who notices a sheen before the city does. East side kitchens often have the opposite problem, a small caddy in a tiny prep room that overflows because pickup was planned for a busier store.

None of that is solved by the hood crew on the roof or by the trap pump in the parking lot, unless someone deliberately combines the stops. Combining the stops is fine. Combining the waste is not. Oil in the trap ruins the point of the interceptor. Oil in the duct is a different accident entirely.

Rules, kept modest

What Atlanta actually cares about, and what this page will not invent

The hard local rule is the sewer one. The Department of Watershed Management grease program tells food businesses to keep fats, oils, and grease out of the collection system, and the city says those discharges are a major cause of blockages and overflows. Pouring fryer oil down a drain fights that program directly. Storage leaks that reach a storm drain are an additional mess, on the street rather than in the sanitary sewer.

There is no Atlanta ordinance that sets your pickup every seven days, pays you by the gallon, or requires a particular tank brand. Those are contract terms. The used cooking oil service page is where the company describes collection and recycling, including monitoring where it is part of the setup. Ask whether monitoring fits your tank. Do not assume it is on every Atlanta stop.

If the interceptor is the thing that is full, go to grease trap cleaning in Atlanta. If the fan is the thing that is overdue, go to hood cleaning. Oil pickup does not close either of those files.

Questions

Atlanta used cooking oil questions

How is used cooking oil different from grease trap waste?

Trap waste is the wastewater sludge pumped from an interceptor. Used cooking oil is the oil rotated out of a fryer. They are stored apart, hauled apart, and counted apart. Dumping fryer oil into the trap to empty a caddy is a good way to overload the interceptor and annoy the Atlanta grease program.

Why not pour it down the drain?

Atlanta's Watershed Management program exists because fats, oils, and grease clog the sewer. A floor drain in the kitchen is on that system. A storm drain in the alley is a separate problem: oil there is an outdoor spill, not a sanitary-sewer question. Keep containers closed and off both.

What does recycling mean here?

TruShine collects the oil and sends it into a recycling stream, which is the service described on the company's used-oil page. This page does not name a plant, promise a payment per gallon, or claim a monitor on every tank. Ask what the current pickup includes for your volume.

How often is pickup in Atlanta?

It follows how hard the fryers run. A Buckhead or West Midtown kitchen with a full fryer bank is not on the same week as a cafe with one fryer. There is no city ordinance on this page that sets a universal oil-pickup day. The FOG permit cares that the oil is not in the sewer. Your fryer care cares that the oil is not sitting rancid in an open caddy.

Do you cover the east side and the close-in suburbs?

Yes for collection routing from the Norcross shop: in-town Atlanta, and often Decatur and Brookhaven on the same day. Marietta, Roswell, and Alpharetta can be added. Say so when you call, because a single Atlanta caddy does not describe a five-store fryer program.
