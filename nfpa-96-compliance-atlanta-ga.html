The standard

NFPA 96 is a rulebook, not a logo

Operators search "NFPA 96 hood cleaning" because an inspector or an insurance reviewer used the name. The standard itself is about the whole cooking ventilation system: capture at the hood, grease removal, duct construction, roof fans, clearances, and the cleaning and inspection of that equipment. A filter wash with no note about the fan is a weak answer to that document.

Georgia's Safety Fire Commissioner adopts the state fire rules in Chapter 120-3-3. TruShine cleans to NFPA 96 practice. Day to day, Atlanta Fire Rescue is who walks into a city kitchen. They are applying the state fire prevention code, including the commercial cooking section, plus any local practice their office uses. If those layers ever disagree, the inspector in the room is the one to ask, not a webpage.

The clock

Georgia's inspection table, as Atlanta kitchens should read it

In the state's fire prevention code based on the 2024 International Fire Code, Section 606 covers commercial cooking systems. Table 606.3.3.1 sets inspection frequency:

  • Solid fuel: every month.
  • High-volume cooking, including 24-hour cooking, charbroiling, or wok cooking: every 3 months.
  • All other cooking operations: every 6 months.
  • Low-volume cooking, such as places of religious worship, seasonal businesses, and senior centers: every 12 months.

The following subsection says that grease found on the hood, filters, fan, duct, or other parts is cleaned. Records of inspection are supposed to name who did it, what they looked at, and when. Cleaning records name who cleaned and when. Keep both on site.

This is a reading of the published state table for restaurant managers. It is not legal advice, and it is not a claim that every Atlanta station has finished adopting the 2024 edition. Confirm the edition before you rely on a row in the table.

The file

What an honest Atlanta compliance visit produces

A scope, written down

The note says whether the canopy, filters, accessible duct, and fan were cleaned, or which of those were skipped and why.

A date

The sticker and the report carry the service date. A blank or recycled sticker is not a record.

Photos

Before and after photos of the canopy and the fan give a manager something to show that is harder to argue with than a sentence.

A separate suppression tag

We do not mark the hood record as if it renewed the wet-chemical system. That system has its own standard and its own vendor tag.

A frequency that matches the menu

A charbroiler and a warming kitchen should not share a six-month assumption. The table above is the starting point. The menu is the correction.

No invented status

We will not tell you the kitchen is certified, ranked, or guaranteed to pass. The inspector decides that on the day they visit.

Questions

NFPA 96 questions from Atlanta kitchens

What is NFPA 96 in plain language?

It is the National Fire Protection Association standard for the ventilation and fire protection of commercial cooking. It covers how hoods, ducts, fans, and related fire protection are built, used, inspected, and maintained. Georgia adopts that subject through the Safety Fire Commissioner's rules and the state fire code.

Does TruShine certify my kitchen to NFPA 96?

No. We clean the exhaust system and write down what was done and when. We do not issue a third-party certification, a star rating, or a letter that says an inspector must pass you.

How often is an Atlanta system inspected under the state table?

Table 606.3.3.1 in the Georgia fire prevention code uses four rows: monthly for solid fuel, every 3 months for high-volume cooking including 24-hour, charbroiling, and wok cooking, every 6 months for other cooking, and every 12 months for low-volume uses such as worship halls, seasonal businesses, and senior centers. Grease found at inspection is cleaned. The fire code official can require more.

What belongs in the file at the restaurant?

A cleaning record with the company name and the date, photos if you have them, and the separate tag for the suppression system. The hood record does not replace the suppression record.

Is the Georgia table a cleaning schedule or an inspection schedule?

As published, it is an inspection frequency. Cleaning follows when grease has accumulated. Most Atlanta restaurants that cook every day clean on that same rhythm so the inspection does not discover a loaded duct. Your inspector's edition controls if the wording differs.
