Atlanta grease traps answer to Watershed Management

The city's own grease page says fats, oils, and grease from restaurants and similar kitchens contribute to more than half of the blockages or overflows in the wastewater collection system. That is why the Grease Management Program exists: permits, maintenance, and monitoring for facilities that discharge to the city sewer. It is a utilities rule, written in Chapter 154. It is not NFPA 96, and a hood sticker does not satisfy it.

Older in-town kitchens, especially downtown and midtown storefronts, often have an indoor trap because there was never room for a parking-lot tank. Newer buildings and pad sites are where you find the large outdoor interceptors. The cleaning method changes with the tank. The reason for the visit does not.

The ordinance

What section 154-297.01 is actually about

Section 154-297.01 is the city's grease trap regulation for food processing, sales, and service establishments. In plain language, if you discharge FOG to the Atlanta sewer, you install a trap big enough to comply, you operate it, and you maintain it. New outdoor traps are described as two 1,500-gallon units in series unless an exception is granted. Indoor traps are sized from the fixture tables in the ordinance, and the text bars you from sending dishwasher water, food-waste-grinder waste, hot water over 140 degrees Fahrenheit, or acid and caustic cleaners through an indoor unit.

The permit comes with fees and inspections through Watershed Management. Plan review for a new trap goes through the city's site development process. Existing traps can be asked for as-built drawings and a field check. None of that is something a hood cleaner invents in a quote. If you are opening, start with the Grease Management Program before you order a tank from a catalog.

Pump-out frequency is the item to verify with your permit. There is no single number of days for every trap, because indoor and outdoor units are not on the same clock and the ordinance text a manager quotes from memory is often the sizing section, not the maintenance section.

The visit

What grease trap cleaning means on this page

Indoor traps

Smaller units under or near the sink. They fill faster. Service is a pump and a clean-out, not a parking-lot excavation.

Outdoor interceptors

Lids off, contents pumped, the baffles checked for a blanket of grease, then closed. Access in a busy lot is scheduled, not assumed.

What we will not dump

Fryer oil does not go in the trap to save a pickup. It belongs in a caddy. See the used-oil page.

A record

Volume and date written down so the permit file has a haul to show. Bring the manifest requirement your inspector already gave you.

Questions

Atlanta grease trap questions

Who regulates grease traps in Atlanta?

The City of Atlanta Department of Watershed Management runs the Grease Management Program for food service facilities that discharge to the city sewer. The fire marshal is the wrong office for a trap question. The ordinance section operators are usually shown is 154-297.01, in the utilities chapter.

What does that ordinance require for a new kitchen?

Food service establishments that send fats, oils, and grease to the city sewer have to install, operate, and maintain a grease trap sized to comply. For new outdoor installations the published ordinance calls for two 1,500-gallon traps in series, 3,000 gallons total, unless the commissioner approves an exception for tight sites, for seating of 100 or fewer, or for an indoor trap when the kitchen has no dishwasher and no food-waste grinder. Confirm your plans with Watershed Management before you build from this summary.

How often does the trap have to be pumped?

Your FOG permit and the ordinance set the interval. Indoor traps are small, so they need service much more often than a large outdoor interceptor. We are not printing a day count on this page because the permit is the controlling document. Ask Watershed Management what your location was approved for, and keep the haul record with that permit.

Is this the same grease as the hood?

No. Hood and duct grease is in the exhaust and is a fire-code issue. Trap grease is wastewater from sinks and floors. Fryer oil should not be dumped into either one. Used oil has its own page.

What do you leave after a pump-out?

A note of what was pumped and when, so you can show the grease program a service record. Disposal is to a receiving site that can take the waste. Confirm the manifest details you need before you rely on a specific form.

Do you cover kitchens outside the city sewer?

This page is for kitchens on the City of Atlanta system. Decatur, Brookhaven, and other cities often have their own sewer utilities and their own FOG rules. Say which city the meter is in when you call (678) 751-8871.
