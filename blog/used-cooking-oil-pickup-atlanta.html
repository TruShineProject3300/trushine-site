Every fryer in your kitchen eventually makes the same waste: used cooking oil. If it sits in an overflowing bin out back, goes down a drain or leaves with a truck you do not know, the problem is yours. Here is how a proper pickup works for an Atlanta restaurant.

How pickup works

  1. A container is placed for your oil: closed, in good shape and sized for how much you change.
  2. A schedule is set by your fryer volume, so the container never gets close to overflowing between visits.
  3. A licensed hauler collects the oil on that schedule and takes it to be recycled.
  4. You get a record of every pickup: date, who collected it and how much.

Why it matters

Oil poured down a drain turns into grease in your lines and your grease trap, and that leads to backups and violations. Oil that spills from a damaged container can reach a storm drain. And since your kitchen made the waste, your kitchen is responsible for where it goes. Used cooking oil can be processed into products such as biodiesel, so recycling it is better than getting rid of it.

Choosing a container spot

  • Close to where the hauler can reach it
  • Away from storm drains
  • Kept clean around the base, since spills create slip hazards
  • Locked or secured if you can

Records to keep

Keep a record of each pickup next to your grease trap and hood cleaning reports. If an inspector, your landlord or your corporate office asks where your oil goes, you can show them in a minute. For what the city's inspector checks on the grease side, see our post on City of Atlanta grease trap requirements.

Watch for oil theft

Used cooking oil has value, so containers are sometimes emptied by someone who is not your hauler. A container that is emptier than it should be, a broken lid or lock, or a pickup with no record are warning signs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does used cooking oil pickup work for restaurants?
A licensed hauler collects oil from your container on a set schedule and recycles it. You get a record of each pickup.
How often should oil be picked up?
It depends on how much oil you change. Set the schedule so the container never gets close to full.
Can used cooking oil go down the drain?
No. It builds up in the lines and the grease trap and can cause backups and FOG violations.
What records should I keep?
The date, the company and the amount for each pickup, kept on site.
