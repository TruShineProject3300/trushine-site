NFPA 96 frequency explained: your schedule depends on how you cook, not on a calendar someone picked years ago.

What NFPA 96 says

NFPA 96 is the national standard for ventilation control and fire protection of commercial cooking operations, and it is the standard fire officials and insurance carriers commonly point to for kitchen exhaust systems. It says the exhaust system has to be inspected for grease buildup by a trained, qualified person on a set schedule, and cleaned when buildup is found. The schedule is based on the type of cooking:

Type of cookingInspection frequency
Solid fuel cooking (wood or charcoal)Monthly
High volume cooking, such as 24 hour operations, charbroiling or wok cookingQuarterly
Moderate volume cookingTwice a year
Low volume cooking, such as churches, day camps, seasonal businesses or senior centersOnce a year

Source: NFPA 96, Schedule of Inspection for Grease Buildup (Table 12.4 in recent editions). Your local authority having jurisdiction decides which edition applies.

Where most Atlanta kitchens land

  • Wings, burgers, fried chicken and anything on a charbroiler: usually quarterly. Fryers and grills run hard and push a lot of grease into the exhaust.
  • Late night and 24 hour kitchens, hotel kitchens with room service: quarterly, because the hood rarely gets a break.
  • Wood fired pizza and barbecue smokers: monthly. Solid fuel builds up fast.
  • Cafes, schools and offices with light cooking: often twice a year.

Your local fire marshal or insurance carrier can ask for more. If you are not sure, start with how much your fryers and grills run each week.

The whole system counts, not just the filters

Clean filters are a good start, but NFPA 96 covers the full exhaust path: the hood, the plenum behind the filters, the ductwork and the rooftop fan. Grease that is out of sight still burns. A proper cleaning removes the grease from every reachable surface, and you get a service record and a sticker on the hood that shows the date and who did the work.

Signs you are overdue

  • Grease dripping from the hood edges or filters
  • A sticky film on the hood that comes back quickly
  • Smoke lingering in the kitchen during service
  • Grease pooling around the rooftop fan
  • No one can find the last service report

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a restaurant hood be cleaned?
NFPA 96 sets the inspection schedule by cooking volume: monthly for solid fuel, quarterly for high volume, twice a year for moderate volume and once a year for low volume. When an inspection finds grease buildup, the system gets cleaned.
Is quarterly hood cleaning required in Georgia?
Quarterly is the NFPA 96 schedule for high volume kitchens, like 24 hour operations, charbroiling and wok cooking. Lighter kitchens can be on a longer schedule. Your fire marshal and insurance carrier can require more.
Does hood cleaning include the ductwork and rooftop fan?
It should. NFPA 96 covers the whole exhaust system. Ask your vendor for photos of the duct and fan, not just the hood.
What does the hood sticker mean?
It shows the date of the last cleaning and who did it. Inspectors look for it, along with your service report.
