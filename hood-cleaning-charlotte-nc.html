Hood, filters, and plenum

Filters are pulled and washed, baffles first. The canopy shell and the plenum behind the filter bank are scraped and degreased down to metal the crew can actually touch. If mesh filters are still in the hood, the report says so, because mesh stores grease instead of shedding it.

Ductwork

Duct cleaning stops at openings that are really there. Where an Uptown riser has no safe panel, the note says that length was left alone. A short run in NoDa or Plaza Midwood is a different building from a tower stack, and the report names which one was opened.

Rooftop fan

If the crew can work the roof, the fan is hinged and the wheel and housing are cleaned. South End screen walls and Uptown setbacks can make the fan the long part of the night. A bright canopy with a still-loaded fan is an unfinished exhaust cleaning.

Line protection

The cookline is masked before any degreaser goes on the hood. Cups and containers that belong to the hood are dumped and wiped. A filter wash is not written up as if the whole exhaust path was opened.

Service tag and report

The kitchen gets a service tag and a written report naming TruShine Services, the service date, and the parts actually opened. That is the NFPA 96 compliant record for the exhaust cleaning. It is not a suppression tag.

Questions

Charlotte hood cleaning FAQ

What does NFPA 96 compliant hood cleaning include in Charlotte?

The hood, the filters, the plenum, whatever ductwork can be opened safely, and the rooftop fan if the roof can be worked. You also leave with a service tag, a written report, and before and after photos. That package is NFPA 96 compliant.

How often should a Charlotte kitchen have the hood cleaned?

NFPA 96 ties the clock to the cooking, not to the ZIP code. Kitchens burning solid fuel are on the short interval, often every month. Lines that charbroil, run a wok, or stay open around the clock are often on a three month interval. A typical restaurant line is often every six months. Seasonal and low volume cooking is often once a year. Grease on the hood, filters, duct, or fan is the reason to clean, and the fire authority at that address can shorten the clock.

Does this visit replace the suppression inspection?

No. This visit removes grease from the exhaust path. The wet chemical system mounted above the cookline is a different appointment, with a different tag. Cleaning the hood leaves that tag where it was. If the suppression tag is the item about to expire, schedule that work by itself.

Do Uptown, South End, and SouthPark hoods get the same scope?

The scope is the same. The buildings are not. Uptown risers are tall, and the roof often requires a building engineer. South End, NoDa, and Plaza Midwood roofs are tighter, and those kitchens usually close later. SouthPark and Ballantyne are closer to retail pads with longer canopies. University City hoods are often large, with a simpler roof walk. The report names what was reached on that visit.
