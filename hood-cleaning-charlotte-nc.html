North Carolina code

Inspection table versus suppression service

Restaurants mix up two clocks. The hood inspection schedule is about checking grease levels on the hood, filters, duct, and fan often enough to know whether grease has to come off. It does not, by itself, set the maintenance interval for the wet-chemical system over the appliances. That system follows its own standard and the manufacturer's instructions. A hood sticker is not a suppression tag.

The 2018 North Carolina Fire Prevention Code put that visual-inspection table at 609.3.3.1. Later state editions may renumber it. Charlotte Fire and the Mecklenburg County Fire Marshal enforce the code the state has adopted for them, and either office can require a tighter cleaning rhythm once they see the menu. Do not hand an inspector a Georgia form and expect the section number to match. The practice is familiar. The citation line is North Carolina's.

We are not on this page claiming a spot on Charlotte's registered fire-equipment list. That registry is for companies servicing extinguishers and cooking suppression systems. Hood cleaning documentation is a different record. Ask us if you also need the suppression visit, and we will point you at that service without blurring the two.

Questions

Charlotte hood cleaning questions

Who inspects a Charlotte restaurant hood?

Inside the city, the Charlotte Fire Department is the place to start. Outside the city limits, the Mecklenburg County Fire Marshal's Office covers much of unincorporated Mecklenburg, and several towns run their own departments. Matthews, Huntersville, Cornelius, Mint Hill, and Pineville should be confirmed one address at a time.

What does North Carolina require for hood cleaning?

The North Carolina Fire Prevention Code applies statewide and points commercial cooking exhaust back to NFPA 96 practice. The hood inspection schedule is a check of grease on the filters, duct, and related parts to decide if cleaning is needed. It is not the service interval for the fire suppression system. In the 2018 code that table was numbered 609.3.3.1. A newer edition may use a different number. Confirm the edition Charlotte is enforcing.

How do the frequencies usually fall?

Solid-fuel cooking is on the shortest clock, commonly monthly. High-volume cooking, including charbroiling, wok, and 24-hour lines, is commonly quarterly. Ordinary restaurants are commonly semiannual. Low-volume halls are commonly annual. Those rows match NFPA 96's familiar schedule and the Georgia table, but your North Carolina inspector's adopted edition is the one that counts.

Is Uptown different from South End?

The code is not. The soil load is. Uptown lunch kitchens and hotel banquet lines spike in the middle of the day. South End, Plaza Midwood, and NoDa run later. Ballantyne and University City are different buildings again, often with easier roofs and longer canopies.

What do you leave behind?

A written record with TruShine Services, the date, and the scope, plus photos. We do not issue a document that renews the suppression system. Charlotte also registers companies that service cooking suppression equipment. That list is about suppression service, not a hood-cleaning license we are claiming here.
